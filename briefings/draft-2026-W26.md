This week's Grid-the-Grey CISO AI Security Brief — the attack surface has moved from your models to your agents, and three stories this week show exactly how that plays out.

Start with AutoJack. Microsoft researchers dissected a three-stage exploit chain in AutoGen Studio where a single malicious web page, visited by a browsing AI agent, reaches the host's local MCP WebSocket and executes arbitrary processes. The chain required no user interaction beyond the agent doing its job. What makes this strategically significant is the attack path: localhost trust bypass, missing authentication on MCP endpoints, and unsanitised command parameters. MITRE ATLAS maps this to LLM Prompt Injection and Insecure Plugin Design. If your developers or analysts are running agentic frameworks locally, they are running exposed RCE surfaces. That is not a development risk — that is an endpoint risk.

Layer on the Vertex AI SDK finding from Unit 42. By pre-registering predictable staging bucket names, attackers intercepted model uploads and substituted malicious pickle files that executed inside Google's serving infrastructure. This is ATLAS supply chain compromise meeting classic pickle deserialization. The model itself became the delivery mechanism. If you have teams uploading models to cloud ML platforms, your CI/CD pipeline needs the same scrutiny as your software supply chain.

Now step back further. Wired is reporting that Anthropic's Mythos frontier model — the one with demonstrated advanced exploit-development capabilities — may have been accessed by a China-linked group. Separately, guardrail bypasses on the consumer-facing version expose Mythos-grade offensive capability to anyone who finds the right prompt sequence. These two facts together define a capability proliferation problem that no single vendor can solve. Frontier exploit-capable models will spread, open-weight releases will follow, and your adversaries' access to automated vulnerability discovery is compressing toward zero friction.

NVIDIA XR AI adds another dimension. Persistent agentic systems embedded in AR glasses, fused with continuous video and audio, connected to enterprise RAG backends and industrial systems. The prompt injection surface is now physical, always on, and worn by your workforce.

The pattern this week is unmistakable. Agents are becoming the attack vector — not just the target. Every agentic deployment is an identity, an endpoint, and a potential command execution surface simultaneously.

This week: audit every MCP endpoint in your agentic deployments and confirm authentication middleware covers those paths explicitly — AutoJack was enabled by a gap most teams would never check. Second, inventory AI agent credentials across your environment; Token Security's research confirms most organisations are running agents on credentials provisioned for other purposes, with no governance layer and no blast radius control.
